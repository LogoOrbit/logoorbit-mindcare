Consistency vs. Correction

Why showing up every day only works when you also check the direction.

Shaista Tariq Founder & Associate Psychologist · PPA Member 22 January 2026 · 1 min read

In short

  • Consistency does not work automatically on its own.
  • Effort without alignment feels productive, until it doesn't.
  • Unreflected repetition often ends in burnout or stagnation.
  • Real progress is steady effort plus a continuously refined path.

The question often comes up: what matters more: consistency in your work, or correcting your direction from time to time? For me, this isn't a debate. People are free to see it from their own perspective.

From my point of view, both consistency and correction are equally important. Consistency only works when it is paired with regular self-correction. Showing up every day matters, but so does checking whether you're moving in the right direction.

Consistency doesn't work automatically on its own. If you keep working without pausing to reflect and adjust your approach, it can lead to a substantial and unreliable waste of energy. Effort without alignment may feel productive, but over time it often results in burnout or stagnation.

Real progress comes from staying consistent while continuously refining the path.

That balance is what turns effort into meaningful growth. Of course, this is my personal perspective, and yours may differ.
